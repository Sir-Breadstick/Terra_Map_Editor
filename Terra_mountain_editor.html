This is a structural terrain experiment, not a tectonics or hydrology simulation. The western terminus contains the existing v0.5 fragmented massifs. The plateau is concentrated around the central section of the belt. The northern fold field contains overlapping mountain trains; the southern side remains one subrange with multiple massifs. The new noise fields are confined to those structures. There is no incision pass. The eastern coast is recorded as a geographic requirement, but no sea or coastline is generated yet.

Whole-range controls

These scale the entire mountain system without replacing your section-by-section elevations and widths.

Sections along the mountain arc

Set the width, northern fold-field breadth, southern subrange breadth, and elevation targets at several positions along the range. The program interpolates smoothly between them. These are example starting values, not assumed facts about your world.

Selected section

Crest values are broad targets, not guaranteed exact maxima after terrain variation. The plateau rises beneath the southern range, reaches its main extent in the central sections, and declines toward both ends. Northern breadth controls a field of several folds, not one ridge.

Terrain character

These controls deform only the chosen mountain subranges, using separate procedural fields constrained by each subrange’s footprint. They do not create river valleys or affect the plateau directly.

What is and isn't modeled

Separate northern and southern mountain generation, central plateau concentration, section widths and elevations, and western fragmentation are explicit inputs. Drainage and erosion are explicitly postponed. The new relief is masked by each subrange, not drawn as arbitrary incised lines. The v0.5 structural foundation comparison excludes its old roughness and synthetic cuts too. Elevations remain schematic and are not georeferenced. The extreme eastern end is coastal in your setting; this prototype leaves the coastline for the continental map layer.
